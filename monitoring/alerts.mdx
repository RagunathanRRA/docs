## What Are Alerts?

Alerts are automated notifications that fire when a pump metric crosses a value you have defined as significant. Rather than watching dashboards continuously, you configure thresholds once and let Samudra notify you the moment a reading leaves the acceptable range — whether that is pressure climbing too high, temperature exceeding safe operating limits, or flow rate dropping below the minimum required for your process.

Alerts do not control the pump directly. They surface information so that operators can take informed action. For hardware-level intervention, see the note at the bottom of this page.

## Creating an Alert Threshold

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Pump Settings">
    From the dashboard, click the pump you want to configure. In the expanded panel, click the **Settings** (gear) icon in the top-right corner of the pump detail view.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Go to the Thresholds Tab">
    Select the **Thresholds** tab from the settings sidebar. Any thresholds already configured for this pump are listed here.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a New Threshold">
    Click **+ Add Threshold**. Choose the metric (Flow Rate, Pressure, or Temperature), set the condition (above or below), enter the trigger value, and select a severity level. Click **Save Threshold** to activate it immediately.
  </Step>
</Steps>

You can add multiple thresholds per metric — for example, a **Warning** at 70 °C and a **Critical** at 85 °C — so that operators receive escalating alerts as conditions worsen.

## Alert Severity Levels

Samudra supports three severity levels, each with distinct visual and behavioral treatment in the dashboard:

| Severity | Color | Typical Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Info** | Blue | Conditions worth noting but not immediately actionable (e.g., pump runtime exceeds 8 hours) |
| **Warning** | Amber | Metric approaching an unsafe range; investigate soon |
| **Critical** | Red | Metric in an unsafe range; immediate operator attention required |

## Notification Channels

Each threshold can deliver notifications through one or more channels:

- **In-Dashboard Banner** — a persistent banner appears at the top of the Samudra interface for every active alert. Banners remain visible until the alert is acknowledged.
- **Email** — Samudra sends a formatted email to the addresses listed under **Settings → Notifications → Email Recipients**. You can specify different recipient lists per severity level.
- **Webhook** — POST a JSON payload to any endpoint you specify, enabling integration with Slack, PagerDuty, or your own incident management system. Configure webhook URLs under **Settings → Notifications → Webhooks**.

## Acknowledging and Dismissing Alerts

When an alert fires, the in-dashboard banner displays the pump name, metric, current value, and threshold that was breached.

- **Acknowledge** — click **Acknowledge** to mark the alert as seen. The banner changes to a muted style to indicate another operator is aware, but the alert remains active until the condition clears.
- **Dismiss** — once the underlying condition has resolved (either automatically when the metric returns to range, or manually after on-site inspection), click **Dismiss** to remove the banner from the active alerts list. Dismissed alerts are preserved in the Alert History.

## Alert History

All past alerts, including their severity, trigger time, resolution time, and acknowledging user, are available under **Dashboard → [Pump] → History → Alerts**. You can filter alert history by date range, severity, and metric. See [Operational History](/monitoring/history) for full details on filtering and export.

## Best Practices for Setting Thresholds

- **Start conservative.** Set initial thresholds wider than you think necessary and narrow them once you have observed the pump's normal operating range over several days.
- **Use two-tier thresholds.** Set a Warning level at ~80 % of the critical limit so operators have time to respond before conditions become dangerous.
- **Review thresholds after maintenance.** If a pump is serviced or modified, its operating characteristics may change. Revisit thresholds to keep them accurate.
- **Avoid alert fatigue.** Too many low-severity alerts cause operators to tune them out. Reserve Info-level alerts for conditions that genuinely require a follow-up action.

<Note>
**Emergency Stop is a hardware control, not an alert.** Alerts notify operators of conditions; they do not stop the pump. To immediately halt a pump from the dashboard, use the Emergency Stop control described in [Emergency Stop](/dashboard/emergency-stop).
</Note>
