<Warning>
**Remote control does not replace on-site safety.** Samudra is a software interface for monitoring and commanding pump equipment. It does not have visibility into physical conditions at your site. Always follow your facility's established safety procedures, permit-to-work systems, and local regulations in addition to the guidance on this page.
</Warning>

## Before You Start a Pump

Before issuing an ON command through Samudra, complete the following pre-start checklist. This is especially important for pumps that have been idle, serviced, or stopped via Emergency Stop.

- [ ] **Confirm no maintenance is in progress.** Verify with site personnel that no one is working on the pump, its pipework, or any connected equipment. Check your facility's lockout/tagout (LOTO) log.
- [ ] **Ensure all access panels and guards are in place.** Physical safety covers must be secured before remote operation.
- [ ] **Check that inlet and outlet valves are in the correct position.** Starting a pump against a closed valve can cause water hammer, overpressure, or equipment damage.
- [ ] **Verify pressure and flow are within safe operating range.** Review the current readings in the Samudra dashboard before issuing the start command. If metrics show `--`, do not start the pump until sensor data is confirmed.
- [ ] **Confirm safe environmental conditions.** Do not start pumps during electrical storms, flooding events, or other hazardous conditions at the installation site.

## Using Emergency Stop Correctly

The **Emergency Stop (E-Stop)** function in Samudra issues an immediate, uncontrolled halt to the pump — bypassing the normal ramp-down sequence.

### When to use Emergency Stop

Use E-Stop only when continued operation presents an **immediate risk** to people, equipment, or the environment. Examples include:

- A person or animal entering a restricted zone near the pump.
- Sudden abnormal noise, vibration, or visible damage observed via site camera or reported by on-site personnel.
- A pipe burst, seal failure, or flooding event in the pump room.
- A fire or electrical fault near the installation.

### What Emergency Stop does

- Commands the pump controller to cut power to the pump motor immediately.
- Logs a **Critical Event** in the pump's Operational History with a precise timestamp.
- Locks the ON/OFF toggle until an operator manually re-enables it.

### Before restarting after an Emergency Stop

1. **Physically attend the site** (or confirm via a trusted on-site representative) that the hazard has been resolved.
2. **Inspect the pump and connected systems** for any damage caused by the abrupt stop.
3. **Clear the area** of all non-essential personnel.
4. **Document the event** in your site's incident log before resuming operation.
5. Only then issue a new **ON** command through the Samudra dashboard.

<Warning>
Never restart a pump following an Emergency Stop using only remote observation. Physical inspection of the site is required before resuming operation.
</Warning>

## Operating in the Field

Many Samudra operators access the dashboard on mobile devices from outdoor or industrial environments. The following tips support safe and reliable field use:

- **Gloved operation:** The Samudra dashboard is optimised for touch input. Use capacitive-compatible gloves, or briefly remove a glove for critical actions such as Emergency Stop. Avoid using the dashboard with bulky insulating gloves that may cause accidental taps.
- **Screen readability in sunlight:** Maximise screen brightness when working outdoors. Position your body to shade the screen where possible. High-contrast display modes can help in direct sunlight.
- **Keep your device charged.** Real-time pump monitoring uses an active WebSocket connection, which drains batteries faster than typical browsing. Carry a portable charger or ensure your device is above 30% battery before beginning a monitoring shift.
- **Be aware of offline mode.** If your mobile signal drops, the dashboard will display a "Connection Lost" banner. Do not issue control commands until connectivity is fully restored and the live data feed resumes. When in doubt, physically verify pump state at the controller.
- **Avoid operating while moving.** Do not attempt to control pumps while walking, driving, or working at height. Find a stable, secure position before interacting with the dashboard.

## Access Control Best Practices

Proper access management reduces the risk of accidental or unauthorised pump operations.

- **Never share your credentials.** Each operator should have their own individual Samudra account. Shared logins make it impossible to attribute actions in the audit log.
- **Use the Viewer role for observers.** Site managers, auditors, or safety officers who need to monitor pumps but should not control them must be assigned the **Viewer** role. Viewers can see all data but cannot issue any commands.
- **Revoke access promptly.** When an operator leaves your organisation or changes roles, an Admin should deactivate their Samudra account immediately via **Settings → Team**.
- **Rotate API keys regularly.** If you use the Samudra API for integrations or automation, rotate API keys at least every 90 days. Go to **Settings → API Keys** to generate a new key and revoke the old one. Never commit API keys to source code repositories.
- **Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA).** MFA is available for all Samudra accounts and is strongly recommended for anyone with Operator or Admin privileges. Enable it in **Settings → Account → Security**.

## Incident Response

If an unexpected event occurs — such as an unplanned Emergency Stop, an unexplained pump restart, a missed alert, or suspected equipment damage — Samudra provides tools to support your investigation and reporting.

1. **Open the Operational History log immediately.** Navigate to the affected pump and click **Operational History**. This log is timestamped and tamper-evident, recording every command, status change, and alert event.
2. **Export the relevant time window.** Use the date range picker to select the period of the incident and export the data as CSV or JSON for inclusion in your incident report.
3. **Review alert delivery receipts.** Under **Settings → Alerts**, check whether the expected notifications were sent and acknowledged. Delivery logs for webhooks are available under **Settings → Webhooks**.
4. **Contact support if hardware damage is suspected.** If you believe a control action from Samudra contributed to physical equipment damage, contact **support@samudra.io** immediately. Include your organization ID, the pump ID, the timestamp of the incident, and your exported log. Our team can provide additional server-side telemetry to assist your investigation.
